## Why is analytics work dividing?

For years, analytics teams were built around a comfortable middle: people who could query data, build dashboards, clean datasets and produce regular reports. That work was valuable because it was skilled and time-consuming. AI is making it neither. Tools now write queries from plain language, build standard charts, draft tests and explain results to non-technical colleagues.

The usual question is whether AI will reduce analytics jobs. It is a fair question, and it misses the more useful one: within analytics, which kind of work is being automated, and which is becoming more valuable? The answer is that the work is dividing between operating tools and shaping decisions.

Demand for analytical work overall is still rising. The [US Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm) projects employment of data scientists to grow by 34% between 2024 and 2034. The growth will not be evenly spread across the two kinds of work.

## What does AI change in analytics, and what does it not change?

AI changes the cost of producing analysis: querying, charting, cleaning and basic modelling are becoming fast and cheap. A request that once took a day can take minutes.

AI does not change who decides what to do with the analysis. It does not set the threshold at which a transaction is held, decide whether a small loss of conversion is worth a reduction in fraud, or take responsibility for that choice. Work moves rather than disappears: from producing outputs to judging and acting on them.

## Find the value: which side of the split is your work on?

Look at what you delivered last month. Tool-operator work produces outputs on request: dashboards, extracts, charts, reports. Decision-shaping work influences a choice: it sets or changes a threshold, recommends an action with its cost, and follows the result.

An illustrative example, not a documented case: an online retailer sees a rise in refund abuse. One analyst builds a detailed dashboard of cases by product, channel and region, and shares it. Another proposes a rule by mid-afternoon: high-value orders from first-time buyers need extra verification, with an estimate of the conversion cost and a review date. Both used the same data; only one shaped a decision.

In my judgement, most analysts do some of both, and the useful question is the direction of travel. The specific tasks being automated are listed in [which analyst tasks AI already automates](/2026-06-30-the-analyst-work-ai-already-automates-and-a-one-week-playbook-to-stay-in-the-room/).

**Decision for you:** what share of your work last month shaped a decision, and what share only produced an output?

## Lead the change: how do you move towards shaping decisions?

The move is gradual and needs a deliberate choice:

- **Attach yourself to a decision.** Choose one recurring decision your analysis informs and offer to own the analytical side of it, including the threshold.
- **Recommend, with costs.** End your work with a recommendation, its expected cost and how you will know whether it worked.
- **Learn the levers.** Understand the systems and processes where decisions are carried out, so your recommendations can be acted on directly.
- **Let tools do the production.** Use AI for queries, charts and summaries, and spend the time saved on judgement.

For managers, the same split means redesigning roles. Give analysts responsibility for decisions and thresholds, connect them directly to the people who act, and measure them by results influenced rather than reports produced. Over time, fewer people will be needed for routine production. Who may act on what analysts find is covered in [who should be allowed to act on an AI signal](/2026-08-20-assign-decision-rights-now-how-to-turn-ai-signals-into-fast-authorized-actions/).

**Decision for you:** which decision will you attach yourself to, and what part of it will you own?

## Prove the impact: how do you show you are shaping decisions?

Keep a record of the decisions you influenced: the threshold you set or changed, the action you recommended, what was decided and what happened. That record shows your move from operator to shaper more clearly than any title.

Review it with your manager. It is the evidence for a role defined by decisions rather than outputs. For a structured view of your current exposure, the [AI Job Risk Analyzer](/ai-job-risk-analyzer/) breaks a role down task by task.

**Decision for you:** how will you record the decisions you shaped, and when will you review that record with your manager?

## What does it cost, and what is the simpler alternative?

Shaping decisions carries more personal risk than producing outputs. Thresholds you set will sometimes prove wrong, and the trade-offs you own will sometimes be unpopular. Some organisations still measure analysts by the volume of reports, which makes the shift harder.

The simpler first step is to add a recommendation and an estimated cost to the next piece of analysis you deliver. It takes little extra time and starts to change what people expect from you. A step-by-step path from operator to decision shaper is described in [how to move from operating tools to shaping decisions](/2026-06-22-how-to-become-a-decision-shaper-in-6-months-a-field-framework-for-ai-driven-ops/).

The split will also change how teams are governed. The important question about an analytical model is no longer only whether it is accurate, but who may change the threshold it uses, how quickly and on what evidence. Analysts who can answer that question for the models they work with are already doing the more valuable half of the job.

## Questions to ask yourself

1. Which of my outputs last month changed a decision?
2. Do I own any threshold, rule or recurring decision?
3. Do I know where and how my recommendations would be carried out?
4. How much of my work could AI produce with a little checking?
5. Which side of the split do I want my career to be on?

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a tool operator and a decision shaper?

A tool operator produces analytical outputs on request: queries, dashboards, charts and reports. A decision shaper uses analysis to influence a choice: setting a threshold, recommending an action, stating its cost and following up on the result. AI is automating much of the first role and increasing the value of the second.

### Are analytics jobs declining?

Not overall. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects employment of data scientists to grow by 34% between 2024 and 2034. The change is in the mix of work within those jobs: routine production is shrinking as a share, while work connecting analysis to decisions is growing.

### What does it mean to own a threshold?

It means being responsible for the cut-off at which a system or team takes action, such as the risk score above which a transaction is held for review. Owning it includes setting it, explaining the trade-off it represents, monitoring its effects and changing it when the evidence says so.

### How should analytics managers respond to this split?

By redesigning roles around decisions rather than outputs: giving analysts responsibility for specific decisions and thresholds, connecting them directly to the people who act, and measuring them by results influenced rather than reports delivered. Managers should also plan for smaller teams producing routine outputs over time.
