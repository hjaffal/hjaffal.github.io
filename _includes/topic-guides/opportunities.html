What is an AI opportunity for a business?

An AI opportunity is a change in what a business can do for its customers or its operations because a capability has become practical. It might make a service accessible to more people, help a team anticipate a loss, or allow people to spend more time on work that needs their expertise. The starting point is the business possibility, followed by a test of whether AI is a useful way to pursue it.

Automating a task is one possibility. Others involve changing the service itself: responding earlier, tailoring support to a customer's situation, or bringing together information that previously took too long to use. A faster task only creates business value if the time, quality, capacity, or customer outcome changes in a useful way.

How to recognize an overlooked opportunity

Look for decisions people make with incomplete or late information, customers whose needs are difficult to serve at the current cost, and work where people repeatedly gather or interpret the same material. Ask the people doing that work what they would change if the constraint disappeared. Their expertise helps distinguish an important problem from an attractive demonstration.

In security and loss prevention, for example, the question may be whether a team can anticipate where intervention will matter instead of reviewing a loss after it happens. Predictive analytics for loss prevention explores that shift. The related breakdown of tasks exposed to automation provides another way to examine the work, while keeping context and accountability in view.

An illustrative example: serving more customers

Consider a service team that spends much of each interaction finding a customer's history. AI-assisted retrieval could make that information easier to use. The opportunity is not simply producing a quicker summary: it is whether the team could resolve more requests while maintaining service quality. Test the usefulness of the retrieved information, errors and rework, customer experience, and what people do with any time released. This is an example of how to frame a test, rather than a claim about a measured implementation.

What should a first test establish?

Define the customer or business outcome, the current baseline, the decision that will change, and the people who will use the output. Compare the proposed use of AI with a simpler process improvement or an existing tool. A bounded test should reveal enough about quality, cost, data access, and adoption to decide whether the opportunity deserves further work.

An opportunity can be real without being ready for investment. If the team cannot act on the information, the required data is unavailable, or the improvement matters little to customers, it may be better to change the approach or stop. The next question is which opportunities deserve investment.
