The question behind the question

Most organisations have run AI pilots. Few can say what they were worth.

The question leaders bring is usually whether the technology works. It is a fair question, and it misses a larger one: whether acting on what the AI produces will change a decision, a cost or a result that matters. I think about it in three stages, and each one ends with a decision for the sponsor.

Find, Lead, Prove: a three-stage approach to AI value

  1. 1 · Find the value

    Where is AI worth investing in?

    Where the value pool sits, how large it is, where it is concentrated, and whether it deserves a structured test at all.

    Decision for the sponsor: is the pool large enough to justify a test, and where should it start?

    {{ topics[0].label }} →{{ topics[1].label }} →

  2. 2 · Lead the change

    Who must work differently?

    Who acts on what the AI produces, who owns each decision, and what has been agreed before the results arrive.

    Decision for the sponsor: who owns the decision the AI informs, and what will they do differently?

    {{ topics[2].label }} →

  3. 3 · Prove the impact

    Did it change the business?

    Whether the AI is reliable, whether acting on it moved a result, compared with what, and what the stop rule is.

    Decision for the sponsor: scale, change course or stop, on the evidence?
