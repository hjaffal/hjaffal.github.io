About

Hasan Jaffal

I write about how leaders in Europe and the Middle East find where AI is worth investing in, lead the change it requires, and prove the value it creates. I live in Luxembourg.

How I think about AI value

Find, Lead, Prove: three stages to AI value.

Most organisations have run AI pilots; few can say what they were worth. I think about the work in three stages. Find where AI can move a result that matters, and size it. Lead the change with the people who own the work. Prove the impact against a baseline, including deciding to stop when the evidence says so.

Each stage ends with a decision for the sponsor. I share the framework, and what I learn from applying it, so that leaders and their teams can use it themselves.

Experience

Analytics, data and product leadership.

My background spans software engineering, product and technology leadership, and analytics in operational environments. My work at Amazon has included security and loss prevention, building an international data science organization, and taking loss forecasting and alerting use cases from proof of value to deployment across regions.

Earlier roles included leading a project management office at ArabiaGIS and product, engineering, data, and content teams at Nabed. Across these settings, the recurring questions have been which problems deserve attention, what people need to change, and how to judge the result.

The standard behind the writing

How I handle evidence.

Every claim on this site is one of three things, and I try to make clear which: a pattern I have seen in my own work, a finding from a named source, or my judgement, stated as judgement. Illustrative examples are labelled as such. Nothing invented is presented as real.

Where the evidence leaves uncertainty, that uncertainty belongs in the discussion. Doing nothing, or a simpler change, is always a fair alternative to compare against.

Other work

Writing and building.

I also like to build. My favourite project is Gepackt, a free study guide for anyone learning Luxembourgish or preparing for the Sproochentest. The name comes from “Ech hunn et gepackt!”, Luxembourgish for “I did it!”, and the whole site is designed to get learners to that moment, one small step at a time.

It brings 16 everyday speaking topics with recordings, nine listening exercises, more than 1,100 words of vocabulary and memory games where every confirmed answer earns XP on a public leaderboard. Learners shape it too: they suggest content and share the speaking questions they remember, so the guide grows with the community that uses it. No ads, no hidden fees.

The Second Mind is my book on how AI is reshaping the way we think, create, and lead. I also publish The Second Mind newsletter for readers who prefer to receive my writing by email.
